Description

The Medical Research Council trial of streptomycin plus bed rest versus bed rest alone in 107 young adults with acute progressive pulmonary tuberculosis, published in 1948 and widely seen as the first modern randomised controlled trial. Patient-level records reconstructed from the published report.

A small, famous trial that is ideal for teaching randomisation, two-arm comparisons and ordinal outcomes.

Variables

streptomycin_tb.csv

One row per patient

Variable Type Description
patient_id string Invented patient identifier
arm string Treatment arm (Streptomycin, Control)
dose_strep_g number Daily dose of streptomycin (g)
dose_PAS_g number Daily dose of para-aminosalicylic acid (g); 0 for everyone in this trial
gender string Sex (M, F)
baseline_condition string General condition at baseline (1_Good, 2_Fair, 3_Poor)
baseline_temp string Maximum evening temperature at baseline, in four bands (degrees F)
baseline_esr string Erythrocyte sedimentation rate at baseline, in four bands (mm per hour)
baseline_cavitation string Cavitation on the baseline chest X-ray (yes, no)
strep_resistance string Streptomycin resistance at 6 months (1_sens_0-8 sensitive, 2_mod_8-99 moderate, 3_resist_100+ resistant)
radiologic_6m string Radiological response on chest X-ray at 6 months, from 1_Death to 6_Considerable_improvement
rad_num number Radiological response at 6 months as a number (1 death to 6 considerable improvement)
improved boolean Improved at 6 months (TRUE if rad_num is 5 or 6)

Known issues

Read these before analysis.

  1. Demonstration entry. Taken from the BioBench R package to show how HDR presents a dataset. It has not gone through HDR review and will be removed before launch.
  2. Reconstructed, not original, records. Rows were rebuilt from the tables of the 1948 article, so the joint distribution of variables may differ from the true data.
  3. strep_resistance and radiologic_6m are measured at 6 months, after treatment, and are not baseline predictors.
  4. No one received PAS in this trial (dose_PAS_g is always 0).
