Description

Notifiable disease cases, public and private health workforce, and target populations for every Moroccan province and prefecture, 2019-2024, extracted from the Ministry of Health annual reports and checked against their printed totals.

Morocco’s Ministry of Health publishes a yearly statistical report, Santé en Chiffres, as a PDF. Its province-level tables are the most detailed public picture of disease surveillance and health workforce in the country, but they are hard to use: tables change from year to year, blank cells stand for zeros, and place names are spelled inconsistently.

This dataset brings four of those tables for the six editions 2019 to 2024 into clean long-format tables, plus one analysis-ready wide table that joins them.

Origin

Produced by Ministère de la Santé et de la Protection Sociale, Kingdom of Morocco

Published in Santé en Chiffres, editions 2019, 2020, 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024

Tables used Communicable diseases (tables 79-84 depending on edition); public doctors by network (table 37); public nurses and technicians by network (tables 39-40); private doctors (tables 41-42); target populations by province.

How obtained Tables were extracted from the PDF reports with scripted parsers (position-based column matching for tables where blank cells stand for zero), then province names and region spellings were harmonised.

Published on HDR HDR-MA-0001, version 1.0.0

Origin address: https://www.sante.gov.ma (accessed 2026-07-20). Reuse terms: Publicly released reports. No reuse licence is stated in the reports; HDR publishes the extracted figures with attribution and does not re-host the reports.

Known issues

Read these before analysis.

  1. The list of reported diseases changed in 2022. 2019-2021 reports separate notifiable diseases and diseases under surveillance; 2022-2024 use one surveillance list. COVID-19 appears only in 2022-2023, meningitis only from 2023, rheumatic carditis only until 2021.
  2. Leprosy 2019 is very likely an artefact. The national rate drops from 37.9 per 100,000 in 2019 to near zero from 2021 and never recovers. Run a sensitivity analysis without 2019.
  3. Food poisoning outbreaks (TIAC) 2020-2021 are approximate. The source leaves zero cells blank in these years; blanks were read as zeros.
  4. Casablanca has two grains. Disease and population tables give one unit; the workforce table gives 8 prefectures. The merged file sums them to one unit.
  5. Population figures are programme targets, not census counts. They are revised between editions (2024 total 36.8 million, lower than 2023 at 37.6 million).
  6. Annex tables differ slightly from headline figures, typically by about 1%, likely different data cut-off dates. This dataset reproduces the annex tables.
